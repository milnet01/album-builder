### The confound, stated plainly

The two arms reviewed **different documents**: 43,823 bytes with seven prior review loops
against 5,985 bytes with none. A larger document costs more to read. This pair cannot
separate workflow from subject, and nothing here claims it does. The honest status of the
draft is *not shown to be cheaper*, which is different from *shown to be dearer*.

A matched pair is chosen and not yet run: **`docs/specs/03-album-switcher.md` (10,775
bytes) and `docs/specs/04-track-selection.md` (10,761 bytes)** — fourteen bytes apart,
both ungated, neither carrying a loop log or a gate record.

### What the pair should be read for, beyond cost

The draft's central cost claim is that naming cross-references beats carrying them. This
run is one piece of adverse evidence: the live lanes, handed pre-executed content, came in
at 71,807–101,289 against the draft's 78,432–162,374, and the cheapest did their whole job
in two tool calls. Looking costs more than reading.

But the draft lanes that went looking produced the `uncited` finding, and the live
workflow has no question for it. So carrying may make a lane cheap at checking what it was
handed, while looking is what finds what nobody handed it. That is a trade, not an error,
and reading the pair for cost alone would miss it.

Two qualifications on the figures above, both against the draft's favour: the lane ranges
overlap more at the bottom end than the headline implies, and the two-tool-call lanes were
loop-2 lanes reading an already-repaired document, so some of that cheapness is the second
read rather than the packet.
